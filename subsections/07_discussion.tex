\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Execution-grounded control planes are the lever.} Tabular agentic QA needs a control plane, not (only) better retrieval. SANA-style ablations~\cite{wang2026lakeqa} idealize search, planning, and execution and find that the residual gap to a solved task is large; that gap is what MOMO targets. The contract abstraction makes \emph{when to commit, when to stop, and when to release a source} first-class concepts in the runtime rather than implicit decisions a single agent must derive from context.

\paragraph{Context isolation and the partial-success protocol generalize.} The combination of bounded workers, compact returns, and enumerated \texttt{missing\_outputs} is the most transferable control-plane idea here. Any agentic workflow whose sub-tasks produce enumerable outputs --- multi-year time series, multi-entity lookups, multi-document summarization, multi-step information extraction --- can adopt the same shape: declare the required outputs in the contract, surface the gap explicitly when partial, and keep raw artifacts inside the worker. Without the protocol, the same partial result either masquerades as success or forces the planner to inspect raw traces to find the gap.

\paragraph{Control-plane primitives expose different cost profiles.} Sprint (single-agent commitment control) and delegation (multi-agent contract control) are alternative levers over the same problem and are mutually exclusive in the flag validator. Sprint adds prompt-section overhead and a few mandatory reflection calls; delegation adds spin-up cost for fresh agents and additional tokens for contracts and returns. They expose different trade-offs between accuracy, cost, interruptiveness, and context hygiene, not strict improvements over each other. Likewise, profiles improve data-environment perception and reduce unnecessary inspection and query calls --- they do not ``make the agent smarter'' in any absolute sense.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Subagent spawning adds latency and cost even when it improves outcomes, and per-task budget calibration is currently manual; the runtime exposes \texttt{budget\_exhausted} as feedback for re-tuning, but does not adaptively reallocate budget mid-run. Source-family selection still depends on planner quality --- a mis-routed inspect contract burns budget the worker cannot redirect. Evaluation is restricted to one benchmark family (LakeQA-derived); whether the control-plane mechanisms transfer cleanly to enterprise warehouses or scientific archives is open.
